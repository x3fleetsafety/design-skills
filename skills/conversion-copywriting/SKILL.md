# Conversion Copywriting

Copy is the highest-leverage design decision. A great headline can 2x conversion. A bad one can 10x it down.

## Core principles

1. **Specificity wins.** "Cut audit prep from 3 weeks to 3 days" > "Save time"
2. **Outcome-led.** What the user gets, not what the product does.
3. **Reader-first language.** "You" 2x more than "we" / "our".
4. **Plain English.** A 12-year-old should be able to read it.
5. **One idea per sentence.** No conjunctions stacking 3 thoughts.

## Headline formulas

### Outcome-led
"[Quantified outcome] in [time period]"
Example: "Cut your CSA score 30% in 12 months"

### Pain-led
"Stop [pain] with [solution category]"
Example: "Stop paying for safety compliance software you can't understand"

### Strikethrough anchor
"Stop paying ~~$old price~~. [Pay something less for an upgrade]."
Example: "Stop paying ~~$130K/year~~ for a Safety Director. Hire the AI one for $79."

### Comparison
"What you'd get from [familiar] for [fraction of cost]"
Example: "What you'd hire from JJ Keller, but $79/month and 10x smarter."

### Question-led
"[Question that reframes the buyer's problem]?"
Example: "Why are you paying $1200/month for compliance software you barely use?"

## Subhead formulas

The subhead extends the headline. One sentence, max two.

Pattern: [Product noun] + [for whom] + [the unique angle].

Example: "X3 Compass is the AI Safety Director for small motor carriers — and the safety consultants who serve them. Every answer cites 49 CFR. $79/month."

## Words to avoid

- **Revolutionary** / **disruptive** — meaningless puffery
- **Powerful** — vague, doesn't communicate
- **World-class** — relative to whose world?
- **Best-in-class** — provable claim that you can't prove
- **Seamless** / **seamlessly** — overused
- **Game-changing** — almost never true
- **Cutting-edge** — almost always cliché
- **AI-powered** — increasingly meaningless

## Words that work

- Specific outcomes ("30% reduction in 12 months")
- Action verbs ("cut", "ship", "catch", "remove", "find")
- Concrete nouns ("driver file", "audit prep", "CSA percentile")
- Real numbers ("$79/month", "100 skills", "10K inspections")
- Comparative ("vs JJ Keller", "vs hiring a Safety Director")

## The PAS formula (problem-agitate-solution)

For longer-form sections:
1. **Problem** — name the buyer's pain
2. **Agitate** — make it tangible
3. **Solution** — your product

Example:
> "DOT audits sneak up. (Problem)
> By the time the letter arrives, you have 60 days to assemble a binder of records you may not even have. (Agitate)
> Compass scans your fleet on day 1 of the notice, drafts every missing document, and assembles the binder. (Solution)"

## Read aloud test

If you can't say the copy in a conversation without sounding like marketing, rewrite.

Example fails: "Our platform leverages cutting-edge AI to optimize your compliance posture."
Example wins: "We use AI to handle your DOT paperwork. Every answer cites the regulation."

## Common mistakes

- Adjective stacking ("powerful, intuitive, seamless platform")
- We-led ("Our product offers..." vs "You get...")
- Long sentences (>20 words)
- Vague outcomes ("save time" with no number)
- Industry jargon without explanation
- Pricing language ("starting at" without ending number)
