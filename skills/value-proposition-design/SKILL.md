# Value Proposition Design

A great value proposition is a single sentence that:
- Names what the product is
- For whom
- The unique angle vs alternatives

If you can't write it in one sentence, the value prop isn't clear.

## The value prop equation

`[Product noun] is the [category] for [audience] — [unique angle].`

Examples:
- "X3 Compass is the AI Virtual Safety Director for small motor carriers — every answer cites 49 CFR."
- "Linear is the issue tracker for product teams who hate Jira."
- "Stripe is the payment infrastructure for the internet."

Simple structure, infinite specificity.

## The 5-second test

Show your homepage to someone for 5 seconds, take it away, ask:
- What is the product?
- Who is it for?
- What's different about it?

If they can't answer all three, your value prop isn't clear.

## "We are the X for Y" pattern

Specific category + specific audience > generic positioning.

| Generic | Specific |
|---|---|
| "AI for compliance" | "AI Safety Director for small motor carriers" |
| "Better project management" | "Issue tracker for product teams who hate Jira" |
| "Customer service software" | "Help desk for SaaS support teams under 50 people" |

## Anti-patterns

- "Revolutionary AI-powered platform" (could be anything)
- "The future of compliance" (when?)
- "World's best X" (unprovable)
- "All-in-one solution" (no focus)
- "We make X easier" (what does "easier" mean?)

## Refining a vague value prop

Steps:
1. Write current value prop
2. List 5 alternative ways the customer could solve the problem
3. Identify what's TRULY unique about your approach (not features — orientation)
4. Rewrite in `Product is the X for Y — Z` format

## Multiple personas

If you have multiple audiences, you may need:
- A primary value prop in the hero
- Audience-specific landing pages for each segment
- Use cases page that segments

Don't try to stuff 3 audiences into one hero — confuses everyone.

## Common mistakes

- Feature-led ("AI-powered, real-time, integrated")
- Audience-vague ("for businesses")
- Hyperbolic ("revolutionary", "best-in-class")
- Industry-jargon ("multi-tenant, API-first, headless")
- 3 sentences trying to cover 5 things
