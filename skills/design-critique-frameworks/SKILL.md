# Design Critique Frameworks

A great design crit makes the design better. A bad one makes the designer defensive.

## Pre-crit setup

The designer should prepare:
1. **Context** — what is this design for? Who's the user?
2. **Constraints** — what's locked? Brand? Tech? Timeline?
3. **Ask** — specific question for the group ("Does the hero work?")
4. **Options** — sometimes show 2-3 directions, not just one

Without these, the crit is unfocused.

## Crit structure (30-60 min)

1. **Designer presents** (5-10 min) — context, ask, walkthrough
2. **Questions of understanding** (5 min) — clarifying only, no opinions
3. **Quiet looking** (3 min) — everyone reviews silently
4. **Feedback round** (15-30 min) — structured (see below)
5. **Designer summary** (5 min) — what they're taking away
6. **Decision** (if needed) — what's next?

## Feedback patterns

### Start-stop-continue
- What should the designer KEEP doing?
- What should they CHANGE?
- What should they CONSIDER doing differently?

### I-like, I-wish, what-if
- I like... (positive observation)
- I wish... (specific change suggestion)
- What if... (open-ended exploration)

### The triangle: user, business, brand
For each major decision, evaluate:
- Does this serve the user?
- Does this serve the business?
- Does this reflect the brand?

## How to give good feedback

- ✓ "The hero copy doesn't tell me what this does in 5 seconds"
- ✗ "The hero is bad"
- ✓ "Have you considered moving the CTA above the trust strip?"
- ✗ "Make the CTA bigger"
- ✓ "I'm confused by how this interacts on mobile"
- ✗ "This looks weird"

Focus on the design, not the designer. Focus on outcomes, not preferences.

## How to receive feedback

- Don't defend until you've heard them out
- Take notes (don't try to remember)
- Ask "tell me more about why" not "but actually..."
- Decide later (not in the room)

## Common mistakes

- Designer presents without context → group asks basics for 20 min
- "I'd do it differently" without saying HOW
- Lead designer gives feedback first → biases the room
- Decisions made in the room when more time / data needed
- No follow-up — feedback ignored or unimplemented
