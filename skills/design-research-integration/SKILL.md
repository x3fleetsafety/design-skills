# Design Research Integration

Research without action is decoration. The goal is decisions, not deliverables.

## When research is critical

| Always research before designing |
|---|
| New product / new audience |
| Major redesign |
| Building for a vertical you don't know |
| Solving a problem that isn't validated |

| Skip research if |
|---|
| You're an active user of your product |
| The change is small / reversible |
| Speed matters more than precision |

## Research methods quick reference

| Method | Best for | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1:1 interviews | Deep understanding of motivations | 1 hr per session |
| Surveys | Validate at scale | Day |
| Usability tests | Watching how users use the product | 30 min |
| Card sorting / tree testing | Information architecture | Async |
| Analytics review | What users ACTUALLY do | Ongoing |
| Diary studies | Long-term behavior patterns | 1-4 weeks |
| Customer support review | What's hurting them today | Ongoing |

## Continuous discovery

Don't do "a research project." Do continuous discovery:
- Weekly user interview (1 hour, recorded)
- Monthly analytics review (which features used, drop-offs)
- Quarterly customer support theme analysis (top 5 issues)

This builds intuition over time.

## Synthesizing research

Common methods:
- Affinity mapping (cluster sticky notes by theme)
- Journey mapping (visualize the user's path)
- Personas (cautiously — easy to oversimplify)
- Jobs-to-be-done (focus on what user is hiring product to do)

## From research to design

For each research finding:
- What did we learn?
- What does it mean for the design?
- What specifically will we change?
- How will we measure if the change worked?

If you skip the last two, the research is decoration.

## Common mistakes

- Doing "research" by surveying employees instead of customers
- Talking to 50 users but synthesizing nothing
- Confirmation bias — research that validates pre-existing decisions
- Personas that nobody uses
- Beautiful research deliverables that don't change the product
