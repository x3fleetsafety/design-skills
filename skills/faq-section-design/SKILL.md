# FAQ Section Design

A great FAQ section preemptively kills objections. A bad one is filler.

## Question selection

Questions should answer the buyer's REAL concerns, not your product's strengths.

Common categories:
1. **Is this legit?** ("Is this legal advice?", "Is my data secure?")
2. **Cost objections** ("Can I cancel?", "What if I outgrow it?")
3. **Comparison** ("How is this different from X?")
4. **Implementation** ("How long until I see value?", "Do I need to integrate anything?")
5. **Team / scope** ("Do all my drivers need accounts?")

Audit your support tickets for question 1-10 most asked. Those are your FAQ.

## Layout patterns

### Accordion (most common)
Questions visible; answers expand on click.

**Pros**: Compact, scannable.
**Cons**: User has to click to see answers.

### Always-open
All questions + answers visible.

**Pros**: All info visible, SEO-friendly.
**Cons**: Long page, fewer questions can be shown.

### 2-column
Questions left, answers right.

**Pros**: Easy scan + answer visible.
**Cons**: Less mobile-friendly.

For most SaaS, **accordion** is default. SEO-heavy pages use **always-open**.

## Answer length

| Length | When |
|---|---|
| 1-2 sentences | Default for most questions |
| 1 paragraph | Nuanced topics (data security, legal disclaimers) |
| 2 paragraphs + link | Deep topics that deserve their own page |

NEVER more than 2 paragraphs in an FAQ answer. Move to a separate page.

## Tone

FAQ should sound like you're talking to a real customer:
- Conversational
- Direct
- Honest about limitations
- Specific (not vague reassurance)

Bad: "Yes, we take security very seriously."
Good: "TLS 1.3 in transit, AES-256 at rest, row-level security at the database level. Privacy policy here."

## Linking from FAQ

Each answer should:
- Link to a deeper resource where applicable
- Link to a related FAQ
- Link to the relevant product page

## Common mistakes

- Q&A that doesn't actually answer real customer questions
- 50 questions (too many — users give up)
- Sales-pitch answers ("Yes! And our product is amazing because...")
- Questions written in PR-speak ("What makes X3 Compass the leading...")
- Answers under 5 words (looks evasive)
- No FAQ section at all
